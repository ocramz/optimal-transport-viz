Where the markers sit

A rotation by angle a has trace 1 + 2 cos a. For a signed permutation the trace only counts the signs on indices the permutation fixes. Cycles fix none, so they sit at 120°. Swaps fix one, so they sit at 90° or on the rim at 180°. The identity fixes all three: the center, or a half-turn on the rim. Opposite rim points are the same rotation.

The axes

ω1, ω2, ω3 are rotation-vector coordinates, centered on the identity. Moving along ωk turns the plane of the other two indices, so it trades mass between those two points while point k keeps its match. A quarter-turn gives their swap.

Twins and traps

E only sees Qij², so flipping the signs of rows or columns leaves it unchanged. The four rotations that share a coupling are therefore the same kind of stationary point. For 60° < θ < 90° the identity is a strict local minimum that is not optimal, and some starts end there.

Cost: three points on the unit circle, targets rotated by θ, squared distances. At 75° this is the post's local-minimum example. The flow uses the Cayley retraction with Armijo backtracking, as in the post.
